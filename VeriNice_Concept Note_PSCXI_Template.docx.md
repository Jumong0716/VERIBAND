 **CONCEPT NOTE**

1. **SUMMARY**  
   VeriBand is a digital patient-identification and medication-safety platform designed to improve patient identification, medication verification, administration documentation, and operational monitoring in healthcare facilities. Rather than replacing existing Hospital Information Systems (HIS) or Electronic Medical Records (EMR), VeriBand is designed as a focused medication-safety layer that connects these processes within a unified workflow.  
   The platform consists of a healthcare-worker mobile application, an administrative web application, a secure patient and medication database, and a soft medical-grade vinyl wristband with a printed QR code. The QR code functions as a secure patient identifier or token and does not directly contain sensitive health information. Authorized healthcare workers can scan the wristband to retrieve the corresponding patient record and access information permitted by their assigned role.  
   A core component of the Minimum Viable Product (MVP) is VeriSense, an AI-assisted operational intelligence feature that analyzes medication-administration records to identify recurring patterns such as delays, frequent exceptions, incomplete documentation, and workflow bottlenecks. VeriSense is intended to support supervisory review and does not diagnose patients, prescribe medications, modify medication orders, or make autonomous clinical decisions.  
   VeriBand seeks ₱500,000 in seed funding to develop and validate the MVP, implement VeriSense, produce initial medical-grade vinyl QR wristbands, establish secure infrastructure, conduct healthcare workflow and usability validation, and prepare for controlled pilot deployment.  
     
2. **BACKGROUND OF THE PROBLEM**  
   Patient safety remains a significant global healthcare concern. According to the World Health Organization (WHO, 2023), approximately 1 in 10 patients experience harm in healthcare, with more than half of this harm considered preventable. Medication-related harm is identified as a major contributor to preventable patient harm, highlighting the importance of strengthening medication-safety practices and patient identification processes.  
   Medication administration requires healthcare workers to accurately identify the patient, verify the appropriate medication and order, administer the medication according to the prescribed instructions, and document the outcome. When these activities depend on separate or largely manual processes, gaps may occur between identification, verification, and documentation. These challenges can become more significant when healthcare workers face high workloads. Nursing workload can also affect medication administration safety. Jin et al. (2023) found an association between nursing workload and medication administration errors, highlighting the importance of efficient and reliable medication-administration workflows.  
   The Philippine healthcare context also demonstrates the relevance of the problem. In a study involving 232 registered nurses in tertiary hospitals in the Philippines, Del Puerto (2024) reported that 72.41% of respondents had encountered medication administration errors. Similarly, Ramos (2024) examined perceptions of medication administration errors among nurses in a tertiary government hospital, further demonstrating the relevance of medication-safety challenges within Philippine healthcare settings. The studies also highlight the importance of strengthening processes that support medication safety, reporting, and monitoring  
   Technology can support these processes when appropriately integrated into healthcare operations. Tan et al. (2026), through a systematic review, found evidence that barcode-assisted medication administration can reduce medication administration errors in non-unit-dose settings. However, technology alone does not eliminate every workflow problem. Healthcare facilities still require practical systems that connect patient identification, medication information, verification, documentation, and operational monitoring.  
   The gap addressed by VeriBand is therefore not simply the absence of digital healthcare systems. Many facilities already use HIS, EMR, or other digital technologies. The opportunity is to provide a focused workflow that connects patient identity with authorized medication information, verification, administration documentation, and operational analysis.  
   VeriBand addresses this gap by functioning as a dedicated medication-safety layer that can complement existing systems. Its VeriSense component further analyzes accumulated administration records to identify recurring operational patterns that may otherwise require extensive manual review.

3. **PROPOSED STARTUP SOLUTION**  
   VeriBand is a digital patient-identification and medication-safety platform that connects five key processes: patient identification, medication information, verification, documentation, and operational intelligence.  
   The physical component is a soft medical-grade vinyl wristband with a printed QR code. The wristband serves as the patient's physical identifier, while the QR code contains only a secure identifier or token that allows an authenticated healthcare worker to retrieve the corresponding patient record. The wristband contains no electronic components, batteries, sensors, RFID, or NFC technology.  
   The platform is organized into five integrated components:  
   

| Component | Function |
| :---: | ----- |
| VeriID | QR-based patient identification |
| VeriMed | Provides authorized medication information |
| VeriCheck | Supports the medication-verification workflow |
| VeriLog | Records medication-administration activities and audit information |
| VeriSense | Uses AI-assisted analysis to identify recurring operational patterns |

   

   The basic workflow is:

   **Patient → Medical-Grade Vinyl QR Wristband → QR Scan → VeriBand Platform → Patient Verification → Medication Verification → Documentation → VeriSense AI**

   Following patient registration, an authorized clerk generates a unique QR identifier and assigns it to the patient's wristband. When an authorized healthcare worker scans the QR code, the platform retrieves the corresponding patient record and displays information according to the user's permissions.

   VeriCheck supports the verification process by presenting relevant patient and medication information before the medication-related action is completed. VeriLog then records the outcome, including applicable statuses such as administered, delayed, withheld, refused, or not administered.

   VeriCheck does not independently determine which medication should be prescribed to a patient. Instead, medication information will come from authorized medication orders or records provided by the participating healthcare facility. The system will present relevant information, such as the patient, medication, dosage, timing, and order information, to support verification before administration.

   Therefore, VeriBand functions as a medication-safety and verification tool rather than an autonomous clinical decision-making system.

   The MVP also includes VeriSense, which analyzes accumulated medication-administration records to identify recurring patterns such as repeated delays, unusually high exception rates, incomplete documentation, and workflow bottlenecks. For example, the system may identify repeated medication-administration delays during selected periods and prompt supervisors to review staffing, workload, or workflow conditions.

   VeriSense is an operational-support tool rather than an autonomous clinical decision-maker. It will not diagnose patients, prescribe medication, recommend medication changes, approve or discontinue medication orders, override healthcare professionals, or independently determine whether medication should be administered.

* **Operational and Patient-Safety Value** – Helps healthcare facilities strengthen medication-related processes while giving administrators and supervisors better visibility into workflow performance.

9. **OPERATIONS PLAN**  
   VeriBand will use a pilot-first, hospital-centered implementation model to ensure that the platform fits actual medication-administration workflows before wider deployment. Operations will cover product development, hospital onboarding, implementation, user training, technical support, monitoring, and continuous improvement.  
     
1. **Product Development and Preparation**

   	The team will develop and maintain the VeriBand mobile application, administrative web platform, secure database, QR-based patient identification system, and VeriSense operational intelligence module. The MVP will initially focus on patient identification, medication verification, administration documentation, user management, and audit logging.

   	The team will also prepare medical-grade vinyl QR wristbands and establish security, access-control, backup, and data-management procedures before pilot deployment.

2. **Hospital Onboarding and Workflow Assessment**

   Selected hospitals will undergo an initial assessment of their existing medication-administration workflow, user requirements, available technology, and integration needs. VeriBand will be configured according to the hospital's approved workflow without replacing its existing HIS or EMR.

   The startup will coordinate with hospital administrators, nursing representatives, IT staff, and patient-safety or quality-management personnel to identify implementation requirements and assign authorized users.

3. **Pilot Deployment**

   Initial deployment will be conducted in selected hospitals, prioritizing facilities in Region 1 and nearby areas. The pilot will introduce VeriBand to a controlled group of authorized healthcare workers and selected patient-care areas.

   The implementation process will include:

   Hospital assessment → System configuration → User account setup → Wristband preparation → User training → Controlled pilot → Monitoring → Evaluation

   Pilot activities will evaluate system usability, workflow compatibility, verification performance, documentation completeness, user adoption, and operational issues.

4. **Training and User Support** 

   Nurses and other authorized healthcare workers will receive training on patient identification, medication verification, documentation, and proper use of the VeriBand application. Supervisors and administrators will receive additional training on dashboards, audit records, exception monitoring, and operational reports.

   The startup will provide onboarding materials, technical assistance, troubleshooting, and post-deployment support to help hospitals maintain effective use of the platform.

5. **Data Security and System Monitoring** 

   VeriBand will implement role-based access, authentication, audit trails, secure data handling, backups, and controlled access to patient-related information. The QR wristband will function as an identifier or secure token and will not directly display sensitive medical information.

   System performance, security events, user activity, and operational exceptions will be monitored to identify technical or workflow issues. VeriSense will analyze available administration records for recurring delays, exceptions, incomplete documentation, and workflow bottlenecks for supervisor review. It will not independently diagnose patients, prescribe medication, or make autonomous clinical decisions.

6. **Evaluation and Continuous Improvement** 

   After each pilot, the team will collect feedback from healthcare workers and hospital administrators and review operational indicators such as documentation completeness, verification workflow, system usability, exception patterns, and user adoption.

   Findings will be used to improve the software, training process, wristband design, dashboards, and implementation procedures before expanding to additional hospitals.

7. **Scaling and Service Delivery** 

   Following successful pilot validation, VeriBand will transition to a recurring B2B SaaS delivery model. Hospitals will subscribe to an appropriate service package based on their requirements, while the startup will provide ongoing software maintenance, security updates, technical support, analytics, wristband supply, and optional integration services.

   Expansion will initially focus on additional hospitals in Region 1 and nearby areas before progressing toward wider adoption across Level 1, Level 2, and Level 3 hospitals nationwide.

8. **Regulatory and Data Privacy**

   VeriBand will be designed in accordance with applicable Philippine data-protection and healthcare requirements, particularly the Data Privacy Act of 2012 (Republic Act No. 10173\) and relevant guidance issued by the National Privacy Commission. Because the platform processes patient-related information, privacy and security will be incorporated into the system design from the beginning rather than treated as an optional feature.

   The platform will implement appropriate technical and organizational safeguards, including authenticated user access, role-based permissions, encryption, audit logging, controlled data retention, and secure handling of patient records. Access to patient information will be limited to authorized healthcare personnel and administrators based on their assigned roles.

   The QR code on the medical-grade vinyl wristband will contain only a secure identifier or token and will not directly display sensitive medical information. When scanned, the identifier will be used to retrieve authorized information through the secured VeriBand platform.

   VeriBand will not independently diagnose patients, prescribe medications, modify medication orders, or make autonomous clinical decisions. Medication information used during verification will originate from authorized medication orders or records provided by the participating healthcare facility or configured within the platform.

   Before pilot deployment, VeriBand will coordinate with participating healthcare facilities regarding institutional policies, data-processing responsibilities, applicable legal bases, security requirements, user access, data retention, and system integration procedures. Compliance requirements will be reviewed and refined as the platform progresses toward actual healthcare deployment.
